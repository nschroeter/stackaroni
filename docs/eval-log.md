# Evaluation log

Running record of experiments against the fixed test stacks in `test-data/`.
Append a new row after any change that could affect output quality — don't
edit or delete old rows, even if the approach was later abandoned; the
history of what didn't work is as useful as what did.

**Also append a row for a change proven *not* to affect quality.** Every rating
here was given to output from the code as it stood at the time, so any change to
a pipeline stage puts every rating above it in question until something shows
otherwise. The proof is what keeps those ratings valid, and it belongs next to
them — a reader asking "does the 5 still apply?" should not have to reconstruct
the answer from commit messages.

**Keep "Current state" below in sync in the same commit as the row that
changes it.** Append-only chronology records what happened but not what is
true now, and past ~15 rows that becomes unreadable: a row can be retracted
two rows later and still read as open. This happened — a halo finding logged
as "needs Human User's rating" stayed that way after being retracted and closed
further down. Updating the block is part of logging, not a follow-up. A row
changes what is live when it: ships a new parameter set, retracts or
supersedes an earlier claim, or resolves an open question.

Score is 1-5 against the quality checklist in `CLAUDE.md`:

1. No visible seams/halos around high-contrast edges (antennae, legs, hair boundaries)
2. No ghosting from misalignment
3. Background bokeh stays smooth, not patchily sharpened
4. Consistent color/exposure across the fused image

Log one row per stack tested if scores differ between stacks (they often
will — e.g. the synthetic stack's thin antenna lines tend to expose halo
problems that the blossom stack won't).

**The defect that removed it: the defocus spread effect (DSE).** A
defocused object's blur spreads past its true silhouette, so at a background pixel just
outside the subject the *defocused* frames carry real signal while the sharp ones carry
flat background — and an activity argmax picks the defocused one, dragging subject colour
outward. Visible as an orange fringe around synthetic_50's ellipse. **`local` is clean at
the same pixels**, and on the only stack with an objective answer beats wavelet 559.6 to
846.1 RMSE against ground truth. **Neither of this repo's named follow-ups fixes it** — the
undecimated transform addresses shift-variance, graph-cut adds a smoothness prior, and this
band is coherent and smooth already. **And the published fix aimed straight at it (Kim 2023,
focus-axis brightness symmetry) was implemented in full and measured inert — see the T16 row.
Three named approaches are now ruled out; read those rows before starting a fourth.**

**Rejected on the way, and worth not re-deriving: rebasing registration onto a canvas every
frame covers.** It removes the streaking too, and Zerene's PMax appears to do exactly that.
It costs field of view equal to half the stack's magnification range — 5.25% on ruler,
5.57% on blossom — which is too much to charge a stack like ruler that has the same
geometry and no visible defect. **The streaking is content-dependent, not
geometry-dependent**: smooth margins expose replicated pixels, textured margins outcompete
them.

**There are now two pipeline shapes, not one (T14).** `core::pipeline::Method` selects
between `Local` — registration → focus → weights → pyramid fusion, the four stages every
rating below was given to — and `Wavelet`, which keeps registration and collapses the other
three into coefficient selection (`docs/algorithms.md` §5). **`Local` is still the default
and is byte-identical**: `output_is_stable` still hashes synthetic_50 to
`0x00455c66dd1e4c95`, so **no rating below is affected**. Wavelet is `--method wavelet` and
a "Method" combo box in the app. **Rated under `wavelet`: ruler 5, synthetic_50 5, and —
after T15 removed the margin streaking found while reviewing it — blossom 5.** Wavelet now
matches `local` on every stack in the set.

**The frame-margin streaking is `warp_frame`'s border clamp reaching fusion, not either
fusion rule's own doing, and it is fixed in T15.** Coloured stripes along the top and right margins, ~230 px in at the sides and ~156
px at top and bottom on blossom. Present in `Local` and `Wavelet` at the same pixels and
the same strength, absent from the source frames, and the difference map between the two
methods is black across the whole affected region. Cause: `register_stack` anchors on the
*middle* frame, so the output canvas is the middle frame's field of view, and every frame
more magnified than it (blossom registers 0.9495..1.0569) must be sampled outside its own
bounds — `warp_frame` border-replicates there, and both selection rules then compete over
the replicated strips. **Zerene's PMax reference does not do this**: full 8664x5784 canvas,
clean to the last pixel, at scale 0.930 relative to ours — i.e. anchored so that every
frame covers the canvas. Fix is T15. **This supersedes the T14 row's "pre-existing warp
behaviour" note**, which was correct that wavelet did not introduce it and silent on why.

**The four-stage decomposition is now known not to be universal.** Wavelet needed a fifth
trait, `StackFusion`, because there is no per-frame `FocusMap` to hand a `WeightEstimator`
and no `WeightMaps` to hand an `ImageFusion` — the quantities live in a coefficient domain
neither type can represent. Registration stayed outside it and is still shared. See the
stage-boundary note in `CLAUDE.md`, which also records that `Select` already re-measures
salience inside `fuse` and uses the weight maps only at the coarsest level.

**What is left untested, and why most of it cannot help where it sits.** Audited against

**Both remaining candidates sit in stages the shipped fusion rule mostly ignores, which is
the T18 finding applied forward.** Focus maps feed the weight stage; under `select`,
weights reach only the *coarsest* pyramid level, because every band-pass level is decided
by `select_more_salient` from the Laplacian coefficients directly — which reads neither the
focus measure nor the weights. Measured: swapping the entire weight family moved 931 pixels
of 1.08M. So a graph-cut weight refiner would refine weights that barely reach the output,
and a better focus measure would feed weights that barely reach the output. **Neither is
worth building against `select` without first changing what consumes them.** The leverage
is in stage 4 — the selection rule itself or its salience measure — which is also where the
7.6-effective-frames figure is actually decided.
